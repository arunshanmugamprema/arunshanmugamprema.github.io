Case study, Bajaj Electricals

Retailer loyalty program redesign

I redesigned a $40M loyalty program so retailers could see what they earn. Participation rose from 15% to 55% within 3 to 6 months, with no extra budget.

A $40M incentive that changed no behavior

Bajaj Electricals reaches over 200,000 retail stores through 350+ field sales representatives. It spent over $40M a year on trade loyalty programs.

Active participation sat at 15%. That means 85% of retailers ignored the program. 35% of high-potential retailers stayed inactive despite rep visits.

Leadership assumed the rewards were too small. The plan was to ask finance for 15–20% more budget.

What I did

I interviewed over 50 store owners and analyzed transactions across all 15 regions. Retailers did not reject the money. They could not follow the mechanics.

  1. Found the calendar friction

    The program ran on thirteen 28-day cycles. They did not match month-end stock counts or retailer cash cycles.

  2. Found the opaque scoring

    Points came from a matrix of 7 variables. A retailer learned the score weeks later, from a printed statement the rep brought.

  3. Moved to a 12-month calendar

    With Product, Finance and Sales leadership, I collapsed the 13 cycles into a standard calendar tied to quarterly milestones.

  4. Gave retailers self-tracking

    An app shows billing progress every week. Qualification now rests on two levers: monthly volume and portfolio width.

The 18-month target, reached within 6 months

We piloted in 2 regions first. That validated the financial model and bounded the payout liability.

Then we rolled out nationally across 350+ sales reps and 15 regions. Area Managers got weekly adoption dashboards.

Participation went from 15% to 55% within 3 to 6 months. SKU billing depth grew 25% and revenue rose 15%, with no added incentive budget.

Before and after

Before

  • 13 irregular 28-day cycles
  • Multi-variable formulas store owners could not verify
  • Reward status only through rep visits
  • Delayed twice-yearly payouts
  • 15% participation, with 35% of target stores inactive

After

  • Standard 12-month calendar
  • Clear volume and product-mix tiers
  • Real-time self-service tracking in the app
  • Predictable monthly and quarterly redemption
  • 55% participation, with inactive target accounts reactivated

When partners ignore an incentive, companies assume the payout is too small. More often the friction is cognitive. If a partner cannot work out what one more order earns, the incentive does not exist.
