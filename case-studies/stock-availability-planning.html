Case study, Bajaj Electricals

Stock availability across 600+ SKUs

I replaced gut-feel ordering with stocking norms based on sales velocity. Monthly sales lost to stockouts fell from 15–25% to near zero within two quarters.

Rational distributors, a failing network

Distributors are rational. They put working capital into fast-moving, low-margin products.

Across 200+ distributors, 40% of the 600+ SKU range was regularly unavailable. Higher-margin mid-tier and premium products were always out of stock.

Retailers kept hearing that products were not available. Within months they stopped recommending Bajaj premium products. An estimated 15–25% of monthly sales went to competitors.

What I did

I did not force blanket minimum orders. I built a stocking model that links the central supply chain to frontline ordering.

  1. Modeled every territory and SKU

    I analyzed sell-through across 50 territories, including seasonal spikes and delivery lead times of 2 to 7 days.

  2. Set buffer stock norms

    Each of the 600+ SKUs got a minimum stock threshold. Fast-movers were capped to stop hoarding. Mid and premium products got mandatory floors.

  3. Closed the loop in weekly reviews

    The thresholds went into ERP ordering. Reps ran weekly distributor health checks on fill rate and stock before taking promotional orders.

Before and after

Before

  • Distributors hoarded fast-movers, and 40% of the range was regularly out of stock
  • 15–25% recurring monthly sales loss
  • Retailers stopped recommending premium SKUs
  • Erratic bullwhip ordering spikes
  • No regional view of shelf health

After

  • Buffer thresholds across all 600+ SKUs
  • Stockout sales loss down from 15–25% to near zero within 2 quarters
  • 15–18% incremental growth in premium product sales
  • Steady replenishment across 200+ distributors
  • Adopted as the corporate standard across 15 regions

Product availability is the quietest revenue leak in commercial operations. Sales teams celebrate big promotional orders while distributors run out of high-margin items. Fix replenishment discipline and the revenue comes back almost immediately.
