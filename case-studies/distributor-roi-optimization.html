Case study, Bajaj Electricals

Distributor ROI and dual-trigger incentives

I tied sales commissions to cash collection, not only to billing. Distributor ROI improved by 25–50% and partner churn fell 18%.

A working-capital problem, treated as a motivation problem

Distributor ROI sat at 18% to 20% a year. Partners needed 24% to 30% to stay invested.

Distributors reported drained cash, late settlements and uncollected market debt. Sales called it a motivation gap. The proposed fix was higher margins and more trade incentives.

I did not ask leadership for more margin. I ran a P&L audit across 100 distributor businesses.

What I did

The audit showed what was choking distributor cash flow. Then I redesigned how the field team gets paid.

  1. Found incentives with no collection accountability

    Reps were paid on invoice volume only. They pushed stock to retailers on 60 to 90 day informal credit. Market outstanding sat at 1.5x to 2.0x monthly sales.

  2. Found a skewed product mix

    Distributors filled quotas with low-margin commodities. They skipped appliances that offered 400 basis points more profit.

  3. Introduced the dual-trigger payout

    I took the redesign to the Vice President of Sales. Commission is still calculated on revenue. It is paid only when collection milestones are verified in the CRM.

  4. Made collection same-day

    Same-day collection with digital receipts gave reps a reason to recover balances fast.

From a 2-territory pilot to a 97-person field team

We piloted in 2 difficult territories. Within 60 days, working capital turnover in the pilot zones rose 40%.

I then led the national rollout across 50 territory managers and a 97-person field organization. We ran range-selling workshops and gave reps real-time aging reports on mobile.

By the second quarter, average distributor ROI was in the 24–30% band. Churn fell 18% and average retailer order value rose 22%.

Before and after

Before

  • Reps paid on invoice value only
  • Distributors carrying 45–60 days of market credit
  • 18–20% partner ROI, with a high risk of defection
  • Bias to low-margin commodity SKUs
  • Manual collection tracking outside the CRM

After

  • Commission depends on verified, timely collection
  • Market outstanding down to 1.2x monthly sales
  • Distributor ROI up 25–50%, into the 24–30% target band
  • Range-selling training lifted average order size 22%
  • 18% lower distributor churn

You cannot fix a channel with margin handouts when its working capital is trapped in unpaid credit. Tie the rep's wallet to the distributor's cash flow, and the field team becomes capital managers.
